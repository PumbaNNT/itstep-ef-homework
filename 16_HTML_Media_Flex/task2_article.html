Cat

The domestic cat is a small carnivorous mammal and the only domesticated member of the cat family. Some cats live in homes as pets, while others are feral and keep away from people. Cats have lived alongside humans for thousands of years, valued as companions and as hunters of mice and rats. Dozens of breeds are recognised today by cat associations.

Cats share their basic anatomy with other felines: a flexible body, quick reflexes, sharp teeth and claws that can be pulled in. They are most active at dawn and dusk. Their hearing picks up sounds that are too quiet or too high for people, and they see much better in low light, although their colour vision is weaker than ours.

Although they hunt alone, cats are social animals. They communicate with many sounds - meowing, purring, trilling, hissing and growling - as well as with body language and scent marks. Kittens are usually born between spring and autumn, often two to five in a litter.
